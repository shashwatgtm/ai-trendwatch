Tech Giants Unite: Agentic AI Foundation Launches with MCP at Its Core

ISSUE #5
DEC 17, 2025
LEAD STORY
The Agentic AI Foundation brings together AI's biggest rivals to build shared infrastructure for autonomous agents. Source: AAIF / Linux Foundation

Something unusual happened this fortnight. OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and Microsoft sat down and agreed on something. These companies spend billions trying to beat each other. Yet they just launched the Agentic AI Foundation to build open standards for AI agents.

At the heart of this is MCP, the Model Context Protocol. I wrote about it earlier as the "USB-C for AI". Anthropic is contributing MCP. Block is contributing its open source agent Goose. OpenAI is contributing AGENTS.md. All under Linux Foundation governance. Bottom line: When competitors collaborate on plumbing, they are signaling that the real war is above the plumbing. Expect an explosion of agentic apps built on these neutral standards.

Disney + OpenAI Sora: $1B IP Licensing Deal

Disney signed a $1 billion IP licensing deal with OpenAI for Sora video generation. This is the largest content-AI partnership to date. Disney gets custom video generation trained on its library. OpenAI gets premium training data and distribution. The signal: Big media is no longer fighting AI. They are cutting deals to control how their IP gets used.

At the same time, 42 state attorneys general sent a warning letter about AI risks in elections and consumer protection. Regulation is no longer theoretical. TRENDS TO WATCH

Rivals Unite on Plumbing: The Agentic Infrastructure Play

OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and Microsoft just donated their proprietary agent protocols to a shared foundation. When competitors collaborate on infrastructure, they are signaling that the real war is above the plumbing. Expect an explosion of agentic apps built on neutral standards. Enterprises will bet on interoperability over vendor lock-in.

First-to-Solve Wins: The "Cinderella Glass Slipper" Effect

a16z data shows AI retention is inverted from SaaS. Launch cohorts retain 40%+ at Month 5 while later cohorts churn fast. The first model to solve a high-value workflow locks in users who build entire pipelines around it. GTM implication: Speed-to-deployment beats feature completeness. Target customers with unsolved workflows, not broad horizontal needs.

Shadow AI Is the New Trojan Horse: 40% of Spend Bypasses Procurement

Menlo data shows 27% of enterprise AI spend comes through individual users. That is 4x traditional SaaS. Add "shadow AI" where people use personal ChatGPT Plus for work, and it hits 40%. B2B implication: Flip your GTM. Target practitioners first. Let usage prove value. Then convert to enterprise. Train sellers to spot shadow AI signals as buying intent.

Programming Is the Canary for Agentic Enterprise Adoption

OpenRouter data shows programming jumped from 11% to 50%+ of all LLM tokens. Average prompts now run 20K+ tokens for code. This is not about coding. It is the signal that multi-step, tool-integrated workflows are going mainstream. If your buyers use AI for code, they are ready for agentic sales, ops, and marketing automation.

47% Convert, 59% Churn: Time-to-Value Is the New Battleground

Enterprise AI buyers convert at nearly 2x SaaS rates. But 59% churn in year one. They are buying with urgency and abandoning when ROI does not show up fast. Seller enablement implication: Sell the implementation plan as hard as the product. Buyers ask "how fast can I see value?" not "what features do you have?" The battle is no longer about closing deals. It is about proving value before renewal conversations start.
