Offbeat AI Watch, Issue 7, 11 November 2026

Your AI can't use what your company only knows in people's heads

Why your team's AI writes generic output, and the controls buyers now want before agents get more access.

By Shashwat Ghosh, AI native fractional CMO

Cover story

The context gap behind generic AI output

Only 10% of large companies have an enterprise business context layer that gives AI a consistent understanding across all systems. TCS surveyed 1,333 senior leaders in 25 countries, at companies with US$500 million to over US$20 billion in revenue. In 11%, business knowledge sits mainly with employees and is hard for AI to reach. In 33%, documentation is fragmented across functions and systems. The top challenge in scaling AI beyond first use cases: data that is not sufficiently integrated, accessible or trusted.

Bottom line: in a GTM team, that context is the ICP, why deals are won and the positioning. It usually lives in the founder's head or in old decks, so the team's AI writes generic output. TCS finds trust breaks most often on unpredictable, inconsistent or inaccurate outputs. Structure the context before you scale the AI.

TCS key findings report (PDF) | TCS Enterprise AI Study | TCS press release, 4 Nov 2026

Editor's note

The TCS Enterprise AI Study puts a number on a gap I usually find in GTM teams: only 10% of large companies give AI a consistent view of the business. For founders, CMOs and ITeS sales heads, that view is your ICP, your win reasons and your positioning. If they sit in one person's head, your AI guesses.

I created the CRAFT framework for this: the five things to tell an AI so its answer is specific, not generic. It is a public skill, craft-context-engineering. Helix's free GTM tools apply the same idea: they turn ICP, positioning and buyer knowledge into structured inputs.

Write down what your best seller knows before you ask AI to sell.

This edition's signal

  1. CrowdStrike puts a human on the AI off switch

    On 3 November CrowdStrike launched an AI Kill Switch. An authorised administrator can stop a rogue agent, block it from relaunching and cut its network access. It works at the gateway now, with the endpoint to follow. CrowdStrike also agreed to acquire Fiddler for agent observability.

    The signal: buyers now expect a stop button and an audit trail.

    CrowdStrike, AI Kill Switch CrowdStrike, Fiddler deal

  2. India sets out how to train AI on data that stays with its owner

    On 31 October India's IT ministry, MeitY, launched its DEPA for AI Training handbook with iSPIRT. It combines contractual permissions, controlled computing environments and privacy-enhancing safeguards, so institutions keep control of their data. “Under DEPA, the model travels to the data,” said Principal Scientific Adviser Ajay Kumar Sood.

    The signal: Indian buyers will ask where your model trains.

    Press Information Bureau

  3. Pliant raises $80 million so AI agents can pay inside workflows

    Pliant, a Berlin B2B payments company, raised an $80 million Series C on 4 November, after passing $100 million in annual recurring revenue. Agents from Claude and ChatGPT to a company's own automations connect over MCP and embed payment workflows, with permissions, controls and auditability built in.

    The signal: agent payments are now a funded B2B category.

    Pliant

  4. UK regulators: an agent's autonomy is no excuse

    On 30 October the CMA, FCA, ICO and Ofcom said existing law covers personal AI agents, from consumer and competition law to data protection, financial services and payments. “An AI agent's autonomy does not diminish the responsibility of organisations that deploy it,” the joint statement says.

    The signal: if your agent sells to UK customers, you own what it does.

    Digital Regulation Cooperation Forum

  5. Anthropic shows enterprises what their AI spend buys

    On 28 October Anthropic made smart reports and pooled group budgets generally available for Claude Enterprise. A smart report samples a team's transcripts and shows the work, the spend and the friction, such as a missing connector. Pooled budgets give a group one shared monthly budget.

    The signal: proof of value per team is becoming part of AI renewals.

    Anthropic

Run through the tool: who owns AI when there is no Chief AI Officer?

TCS page 24 finds 37% of companies have a Chief AI Officer; 96% of those give it primary responsibility for scaling AI. Without one: CIO 30%, CTO 29%, CEO 16%, COO 7%, Chief Data Officer 7%. Input: those six titles, an enterprise AI product, companies with US$500 million to over US$20 billion revenue.

ICP Intelligence, buyer_group_analyzer: output lines, quoted
“Authority unconfirmed: Chief AI Officer; Chief Data Officer.”
“Also in the group (your input): Chief Technology Officer (technical evaluation).”
“Usual in AI native, not in your list: Business owner of the process the AI changes (budget or sign-off).”

How I read it: a title is not a budget. Without a CAIO, win the CIO and CTO on data access and find the process owner who signs.

Builder's corner: your next user may be an AI agent

Microsoft made its Dynamics 365 sales MCP server generally available on 29 October (Microsoft). 59 governed actions, from leads to quotes and orders, now work inside Cowork, GitHub Copilot or any MCP assistant. Access follows each user's CRM permissions. Microsoft calls it “headless CRM”.

The takeaway: big vendors are keeping their APIs and adding MCP on top. If a buyer's AI cannot reach your product, you drop out of the workflow. Helix built its 48 free GTM tools this way. Try them.

Offbeat trends of the fortnight

  1. Companies buy control before autonomy

    Two thirds of large companies let AI make decisions nowhere or in only a few use cases, TCS finds. 32% rate exceptions and human intervention highly effective; 9% say that of real-time monitoring. CrowdStrike's kill switch puts a person on the trigger. Microsoft's MCP tools run under each user's CRM permissions. What this means for you: lead every AI pitch with the human override.

  2. The data stays put and the AI comes to it

    Under India's DEPA framework, the model travels to the data. Microsoft's sales MCP server keeps agent access inside each user's CRM roles. And 43% of leaders in Continental Europe rate AI sovereignty requirements a high or very high risk, TCS finds. What this means for you: prepare a one-page answer on where your AI trains, runs and keeps data.

  3. AI agents get spending power, and accountability comes with it

    Pliant lets agents connect over MCP and pay inside business workflows, with permissions, controls and audit built in. Microsoft's sales MCP tools let agents create quotes and turn them into orders. The UK regulators say the firm that deploys an agent stays responsible for it. What this means for you: name who signs off each payment, quote or order your agent can start.
