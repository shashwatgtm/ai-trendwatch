Your AI doesn't know your business

Only 10% of large companies give AI one view of the business. That is why your team's AI sounds generic.

By Shashwat Ghosh, AI native fractional CMO

Cover story

The context gap

Only 10% of large companies give AI one consistent view of the business, a TCS survey of 1,333 senior leaders finds. In 44%, the knowledge sits with employees (11%) or in scattered documents (33%). The top barrier to scaling AI: data that is not integrated, accessible or trusted.

Bottom line: in a GTM team, that context is your ICP, your win reasons and your positioning. If it lives in the founder's head, your AI writes generic output. Structure the context before you scale the AI.

Offbeat news

  • CrowdStrike adds an AI off switch

    An administrator can now stop a rogue agent, block it from relaunching and cut its network access. Gateway first, endpoint next. CrowdStrike is also buying Fiddler for agent observability. The signal: Buyers now expect a stop button and an audit trail. SC Media · Fiddler deal

  • India: train AI where the data lives

    MeitY's DEPA for AI Training handbook lets institutions keep control of the data used to train AI. “Under DEPA, the model travels to the data,” says Principal Scientific Adviser Ajay Kumar Sood. The signal: Indian buyers will ask where your model trains. PIB

  • Pliant: $80M for agent payments

    The Berlin B2B payments firm raised a Series C after passing $100M ARR. Agents from Claude, ChatGPT or in-house automations connect over MCP and pay inside workflows, with permissions and audit built in. The signal: Agent payments are now a funded B2B category. Pliant

  • UK: you own what your agent does

    The CMA, FCA, ICO and Ofcom say existing law already covers AI agents. “An AI agent's autonomy does not diminish the responsibility of organisations that deploy it.” The signal: Selling to UK customers through an agent? You are accountable. DRCF

  • Anthropic shows what AI spend buys

    Claude Enterprise now has smart reports, a sampled view of each team's work, spend and friction, plus pooled monthly budgets for groups. The signal: Proof of value per team is coming to AI renewals. Anthropic

Offbeat trends of the fortnight

  1. 01

    Control before autonomy

    Two thirds of large companies let AI decide nowhere or in only a few cases (TCS). CrowdStrike puts a person on the kill switch; Microsoft's agent tools follow CRM permissions.

    What this means for you: Lead every AI pitch with the human override.

  2. 02

    The AI goes to the data

    DEPA: the model travels to the data. Microsoft: agents see only what each user's CRM role allows. TCS: 43% in Continental Europe call AI sovereignty a high risk.

    What this means for you: Keep a one-page answer on where your AI trains, runs and stores data.

  3. 03

    Agents get a budget, and an owner

    Pliant lets agents pay inside workflows. Microsoft's agents can turn quotes into orders. UK regulators say the deploying firm stays responsible.

    What this means for you: Name who signs off every payment, quote or order your agent can start.

What to ask a CIO before you sell AI

I gave our discovery question tool the TCS findings above as the buyer's pain, a CIO on a first call and an AI agent that drafts sales content. Three of the questions it returned:

  • “Where does the data it needs live, and what may leave your environment?”
  • “How do you judge that a result is right today: who reviews it, and against what?”
  • “Which workloads or tasks would you move first, and which never?”

Builder's corner

Your next user may be an AI agent

Microsoft's Dynamics 365 sales MCP server is generally available (Microsoft, 29 Oct): 59 governed tools, from leads to quotes and orders, inside any MCP assistant, under each user's CRM permissions.

Microsoft's reasoning: “Sellers don’t live in one place. They work across chat, email, agent-driven workflows, and the tools closest to the deal, so CRM needs to meet them there.” It calls this a step toward headless CRM.

Takeaway: big vendors are adding MCP on top of their APIs. If a buyer's AI cannot reach your product, you drop out of the workflow. Helix built its 48 free GTM tools this way.
